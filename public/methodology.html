How it's measured

Detection is fully deterministic. There are no LLM judges anywhere in the pipeline — every number here can be reproduced by re-reading the same answers, which you can do yourself via View raw data on any search term. Each run asks all five engines the same question and reads their answers the same way.

How a scan works

Every step is deterministic. Brands are logged from citations, non-vendor domains are filtered, and each brand's move since the last run is recorded.

Mention vs. citation

These are tracked separately because they are different kinds of visibility. A brand can be cited as a source without ever being recommended, and it can be recommended without its own site being linked.

  • Mention — the engine named the brand in its answer text. A brand counts as mentioned when one of its names appears on a word boundary in the answer, after citation link URLs are stripped first (so a domain sitting inside a citation is never mistaken for a prose mention).
  • Citation — the engine linked the brand's own domain as a source. A brand counts as cited when one of the sources the engine links resolves to the brand's registrable domain.

Sampling

AI answers are non-deterministic — ask the same question twice and the brands can change. So each engine is queried several times per scan (three by default) and the results are averaged: within each engine first (the share of its samples that named or cited the brand), then across the engines that answered, so every engine carries equal weight regardless of how many samples it returned.

Averaging across scans

Sampling within a scan is not enough on its own. A brand's mention rate is a proportion estimated from a limited number of draws (5 engines × 5 samples = 25 answers at the default), so it carries real sampling error — and that error is largest for brands in the middle of the board, which is exactly where most brands sit. Measured on live scans, a brand averaging an 18% mention rate moved between 4.9 and 24.8 points across scans taken minutes apart, with nothing underneath it changing. The observed spread matched what binomial sampling noise predicts, engine by engine, so it was noise rather than movement.

So the AI Visibility score, mention rate, citation rate, and position factor shown on the leaderboard are averaged over the last three scans. That cuts the run-to-run standard deviation by roughly 2.8×. Matching the same reduction by sampling harder would take about 24× as many API requests per scan. Each row also shows its own single-scan value underneath, so nothing is hidden, and the trend line uses the same trailing average as the headline number.

The trade-off is deliberate: a genuine change in visibility takes about three scans to show up in full rather than appearing instantly. Averaging only counts scans in which a brand was already on the board, so a newly-discovered brand starts unsmoothed and settles as the window fills.

Do the engines agree?

The matrix under the leaderboard shows, for each brand, what each engine did with it. A cell's percentage is that engine's mention rate — the share of its samples that named the brand — so you'll see values like 67% (named in two of three samples). A ◆ marks that the engine also cited the brand as a source. The Named by column counts how many of the five engines named it at least once, so you can tell a brand every engine agrees on from one that only a single engine likes.

The AI Visibility Score

A single 0–100 number per brand, per search term, computed across that term's engine answers (averaged over the samples above, then over the last three scans).

score = 100 × ( 0.70 × mention rate + 0.15 × citation rate + 0.15 × position factor )
70%
Mention rate — the share of answers that mention the brand.
15%
Citation rate — the share of answers that cite the brand's domain.
15%
Position factor — the mean of 1/position over answers that mention it. Being named first counts more than being named fifth.

Share of voice

A brand's share of all brand mentions for the search term in that run. If ten brands are named twenty times in total and one brand accounts for five of those, its share of voice is 25%.

The brand log

Brands are discovered from the sources the engines cite: any newly-cited domain is added to that term's log and stays there permanently. Once a brand is in the log, it is matched by name across every term. This is why a brand keeps its place on the leaderboard even after it stops showing — so you can watch it move. Your own brand is always included, whether it shows or not.

Each brand is identified by its registrable domain (taken from the real citation link), so the same brand never appears twice under different spellings. Two different companies that share a name but sit on different domains (say serpapi.com and serpapi.cc) are kept as separate brands. When the answer text just names the shared word — which on its own is ambiguous — the mention is credited to one of them: the brand whose domain the engine cited in that answer, or otherwise the primary (the most-cited across the run). That way a look-alike domain can't inherit the real company's score. Only companies that sell a product or service belong on a board, so search engines, forums, encyclopedias, code hosts, and review/comparison sites are filtered out by a curated denylist — and you can exclude anything it misses, or add a brand it missed, straight from the term's pages. None of this uses an LLM; it's a maintained list, so the results stay reproducible.

Movement

The change in a brand's leaderboard position since the previous run. ▲ means it climbed, ▼ means it slipped, and NEW marks a brand appearing on the board for the first time.

Engines: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, and Google AI Mode, all queried through SearchApi.io. Each engine is sampled three times per term per run, and the samples are averaged.
